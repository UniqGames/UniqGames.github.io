The game

One rule, hundreds of ways to break it

Every level is a set of glass jars stacked with mixed-up candy. Tap one jar, tap another, and the run of matching colours pours across. Fill every jar with a single colour and the level is done. Simple to learn, and quietly ruthless once the harder pieces arrive.

Character design

Meet the crew

The puzzles would work perfectly well without them. They would also be extremely boring.

The sceptic

Tastes everything twice

Strong opinions about candy, and no talent whatsoever for keeping them quiet. No jar is finished until it has been inspected, frowned at and — usually — sampled, purely to be certain.

The optimist

Solves it with enthusiasm

Convinced that every problem gives way to a little more enthusiasm. Roughly half the time this is true. The other half is how strawberry ended up in places strawberry does not belong.

The inventor

Tunes in distant recipes

The antenna is not decorative — it picks up recipes from somewhere far away, apparently. That would explain the mystery candy, the curtain jars and several contraptions nobody asked for.

World design

Built from glass, sugar and bad ideas

Soft shapes, saturated colour and a lot of gloss. Everything on screen is meant to look good enough to eat and clear enough to read at a glance on a phone.

Match items

Fourteen candy colours, each with its own silhouette in the jar. They are deliberately distinct in brightness as well as hue, so the board stays readable for colour-blind players.

Power-ups and boosters

Four tools, a handful of free charges per level, and the option to buy more with the gold you earn. Used well, they turn a dead board into a clean finish.

Undo

Take back the pour you immediately regretted. The most-used booster in the game, by a wide margin.

Shuffle

Redistribute the board when the layout has painted you into a corner.

Hint

Highlights a move that actually leads somewhere, for the moments the answer refuses to appear.

Add jar

Buy one more empty jar with gold. Expensive, and occasionally the only thing that saves a level.

Game elements

New machinery arrives as you climb, so the same core rule keeps producing new problems for hundreds of levels.

Mystery candy

Arrives around level 12. You cannot see its colour until the candy above it moves, so every plan carries a little risk.

Curtain jars

From level 20, some jars stay hidden behind a curtain until you finish the colour they are waiting on.

Stone jars

Later levels bolt jars into rock. They cannot be moved, only worked around — and they change the shape of every solution.

Areas

Levels are grouped into destinations along a journey map. Each one has its own scenery, its own mix of machinery, and a small celebration when you finish it.

Holiday Road

Where it all begins. Wide-open boards that teach the rules without ever saying so out loud.

Treasure Island

Curtain jars show up and the board stops telling you everything. Candy Cove, Frosty Peaks, Sunny Meadows and Mystic Forest follow.

Events and community

Better with other monsters

Sorting is a solo act. Bragging is not.

Weekly leaderboard

Every level you clear adds to a weekly score. The board resets on a schedule, so a bad week is only ever a week — and a good one puts you in front of players who have been at it far longer.

Teams

Join a team, share lives when someone is stuck, and take on goals together. It is the part of the game that runs on other people rather than sugar. Teams unlock at level 39, the weekly leaderboard at level 40.
